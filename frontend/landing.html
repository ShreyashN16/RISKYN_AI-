The problem

Fraud, returns, and chargebacks quietly eat margin

Most detectors either flag everything (drowning analysts in noise) or stay quiet until a chargeback lands. Both are expensive — just in different currencies: analyst hours or written-off revenue.

Fraud spikes

Velocity bursts and amount anomalies that look like a compromised account acting fast, before a human notices.

Coordinated abuse rings

Mule networks that share a device or funnel into one receiver — invisible transaction-by-transaction, obvious as a graph.

Chargeback disputes

Evidence that exists but is scattered — sender history, device signals, prior clean behavior — never assembled fast enough.

The approach

Bounded AI authority, not autonomous AI

Every decision band has an explicit ceiling on what the AI is allowed to do. Nothing is implicit.

01

Score, explainably

Five interpretable rule signals fused with an unsupervised anomaly model into one 0–100 score — never a black box.

02

Prove it on unseen data

A genuine 60/20/20 train/val/test split. The threshold is tuned on validation only; the precision and recall you see are computed on data the model never touched.

03

Recommend, don't act

ALLOW, STEP-UP VERIFY, or BLOCK-AND-REVIEW — the ceiling of AI authority at every band is published in the Policy Gate view, not buried in code.

04

Explain in plain English

Every flagged transaction gets a written reasoning trace — a template by default, upgradeable to a real LLM call if you supply a key.

05

Log everything

Every training run, flag, and evidence packet is timestamped in an audit trail — because "trust us" isn't a compliance answer.
